# High-Intent SEO Opening Lines

Write the *opening* of a page so it answers the searcher's intent immediately —
preserving rankings and converting the visit. The opening does almost all the work; with
a clear-enough opening the searcher doesn't need to read the rest.

**Core principle:** answer the search intent right away — no scrolling. Two reasons:
(1) it stops **pogo-sticking** (returning to the SERP to try another result), and
(2) it captures the visit (convert a high-intent buyer now, or move an informational
reader down the funnel). Get it wrong → missed sales first, then weaker user signals
and harder SEO.

## Step 0 — Classify intent and funnel stage (do this before writing)
- **Search intent:** the reason behind the query. The practical split: **informational** (top of funnel — "looking for an answer, then gone") vs **high/transactional** (bottom of funnel — "knows what they want, choosing a brand"). One question: does the searcher already know what they want, or are they looking for an answer?
- **TOFU** = broad, high volume, low intent, more "at-bats," harder to convert. **BOFU** = narrow, lower volume, high purchase intent. *BOFU trips up more writers than TOFU* — most mistakes happen on the pages closest to the sale.
- **Keyword:** put it (or a close natural variation) at the start of the first sentence — it tells the searcher "this is for you." For a long-tail, *uncompetitive* phrase that's awkward to use verbatim, a close variation keeps the relevance.

## Playbook A — Top of funnel (informational)
Job: answer fast, earn trust, then move them down the funnel.
1. Keyword (or close variation) at the start of the first sentence.
2. Answer the question immediately.
3. Give the solution / easiest fix.
4. Expand each point in depth *afterward* — trust grows as they read.
5. Move them down the funnel (lead magnet, newsletter, product, service).
- *Recommended:* add a 2–3 sentence **TL;DR** at the very top (reported +33% conversions in a cited case).

**Example — keyword "why is my sink draining slowly":**
> If you're wondering why your sink is draining slowly, it's probably a buildup somewhere in the pipe. The most common causes are hair, grease, soap scum, food particles, or mineral deposits restricting flow. The easiest fix is usually flushing with hot water, using a plunger, or running a small drain snake through the pipe.

States the answer, then causes, then the easiest fix — in ~two sentences. Causes get
expanded later.

## Playbook B — Bottom of funnel (high intent)
The searcher knows what they want and is choosing a brand. Open by answering **"why
choose you"** — do *not* explain what the category is; they already know.
1. **Make the brand the subject** — "We are…" / "Our [product]…", not an abstract service ("[Category] is designed to…"). A clear subject reads like a real business.
2. Work the keyword in naturally.
3. Build confidence in *you* — concrete services, speed, availability, trust language ("homeowners rely on").
4. Use emotionally compelling outcomes — "prevent **costly** water damage", not "prevent water damage".
5. Move to the CTA and remove friction ("Use free, no signup required" + a "Use now" button).

Describing what the service does is fine — but from a *sales* angle ("this is what we
do"), because the searcher is looking to be sold.

**Example — keyword "24-hour plumber Hoboken":**
> *Anti-pattern (informational voice):* "24-hour plumber Hoboken services are designed to handle emergency plumbing problems like burst pipes, clogged drains…" — explains what the searcher already knows; reads like Wikipedia, not a business.
> *Pattern:* "**We are** a 24-hour plumber Hoboken homeowners rely on for fast emergency repairs to leaks, burst pipes, clogged drains, and water heater issues, so you can prevent **costly** water damage and restore your plumbing quickly, even when other plumbers are closed."

**Example — keyword "room planner app":**
> *Pattern:* "**Our** room planner app uses AI to measure your room and create furniture layouts instantly from your photos. Upload two pictures and let the AI generate dimensions, placement ideas, and design layouts in minutes. Use free with no signup required. [Use now]"

## Checklist (required = pass/fail, recommended = quality)
**Intent** — keyword identified (req); funnel stage classified (req).
**Answer** — intent answered in the first 1–2 sentences, no scrolling (req); no
definition of what the searcher already knows, no company-history preamble (req);
keyword or close variation in sentence 1 (rec); TOFU TL;DR or product features/benefits
summary up top (rec).
**Voice — TOFU** — sentence 1 echoes the query → answer → easiest fix (req); depth after
the quick answer (rec).
**Voice — BOFU** — brand is the subject (req); opening answers "why choose you," not
"what the category is" (req); a concrete differentiator named (rec); an emotional outcome (rec).
**Action** — BOFU: CTA present and reachable without scrolling (req); friction removed (rec);
TOFU: a next step into the funnel (rec).

## Red flags — rewrite the opening if present
- Informational voice on a high-intent keyword ("[Category] is designed to…") — the most common and most costly mistake.
- The answer is below the fold.
- An abstract subject ("Services are provided…") instead of a clear "we."
- No CTA, or it's buried.

## A note on the ranking claims
The source treats pogo-sticking as directly lowering rankings. The external consensus is
more guarded — Google has said pogo-sticking/bounce aren't *direct* ranking factors,
though behavioral satisfaction may feed in indirectly. The copywriting implication is
unchanged: an immediate, intent-matching answer reduces return-to-SERP behavior and
improves the visit either way.
