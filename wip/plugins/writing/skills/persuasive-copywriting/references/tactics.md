# Psychology Tactics (Nick Kolenda)

35 evidence-backed tactics across four families. Each is a documented cognitive effect,
not a style preference. Apply deliberately — pick the tactic that fits the goal, then
phrase for it. Citations point to the studies behind each (full bibliography on the
source site's `/references`).

## Framing (16) — change perception by changing presentation
The facts don't move; framing decides which facts are salient and what reference point
the reader compares against. "Half a pound of fat lost" and "you'll never fit those
jeans again" are the same scale reading, not the same pitch.

**Scarcity & social proof**
- **Choose the right type of scarcity.** Supply scarcity ("limited edition") = status symbol → works for conspicuous/luxury goods. Quantity scarcity ("only 2 left") = high demand → works for rational products. Time scarcity (seasonality, deadlines) = motivation to start → works for indulgences and large purchases. (Barton 2022; Jang 2015)
- **Mention the growing popularity of a behavior.** Rising trends are more persuasive than static majorities — people follow momentum, not just the crowd.

**Simulation & imagination** — make the outcome easy to picture
- **Help customers simulate a purchase.** Prompt the reader to mentally rehearse owning/using it; simulation drives desire.
- **Write copy that is easy to imagine.** Concrete, sensory, low-abstraction language; vivid copy is processed as more real.
- **Make hypothetical actions more vivid.** Spell out the imagined scenario in detail so it feels experienced, not theorized.
- **Depict the first step as completed.** Endowed progress — show the reader already partway (e.g. a loyalty card with the first stamps filled) and they push to finish.

**Beneficiary & identity** — aim the benefit at the right person
- **Describe impacts on other people.** Beneficiary framing — benefits to *others* can motivate more than self-benefit in the right context.
- **Specialize for roles, not actions.** "For marketers" beats "for marketing"; identity-based framing recruits self-concept.
- **Insert customer names into purchases.** Personalization (the name) raises ownership and relevance.
- **Tailor your pitch to individual scenarios.** Match the copy to the reader's specific situation; specificity raises fit and trust.

**Numbers & evidence** — frame the proof itself
- **Digits convert higher than words.** Write "7", not "seven" — numerals stop the eye and read as more precise/credible.
- **Avoid meaningless 100% claims.** A round "100%" can read as hype/empty; precise figures are more believable.
- **Don't use science to sell emotional products.** Rational/scientific framing can *backfire* on hedonic products — it dampens the feeling that drives the buy.

**Positioning & presentation**
- **Describe your product with directional consistency.** Keep metaphoric direction coherent (up = more/better) so the frame doesn't fight itself.
- **Reframe products to be chronologically newer.** "New/latest" framing raises perceived value.
- **Remove empty space below sales copy.** Whitespace under the copy can signal "the page is over"; closing it keeps the reader moving toward the action.

## Linguistics (7) — words, sounds, grammar below the level of argument
Two sentences with identical claims read differently because of tense, sound, or word
choice. These tune sub-conscious **fluency** and **simulation** signals so the *form*
reinforces the *meaning*. (Psycholinguistic findings — processing fluency, mental imagery, construal.)
- **Align the semantic and linguistic meaning.** Make an easy process read in an easy sentence — fluency of the sentence transfers to the product.
- **Build and expand the same mental image.** Keep one coherent picture in the reader's head rather than switching metaphors.
- **Depict information with positive frames.** "Leak-proof", not "doesn't leak" — positive frames are easier to process and sound more confident.
- **Diversify the flow of words and sounds.** Vary rhythm and phonemes for readability and pleasantness.
- **End positive statements with emojis.** Supplemental, facial, positive — reinforces affect on upbeat lines.
- **List good actions in the present tense.** Present tense feels ongoing and more valuable ("we deliver", not "we delivered").
- **Remove meaningless words from products.** Buzzwords raise *price* expectations but lower *quality* perception — cut them. (See `scripts/scan_markers.py` brand-prosody flags.)

## Calls to Action (8) — make the act feel easy, natural, worth taking
The click is shaped by **motor simulation and fluency.** (Embodied cognition, fluency, choice architecture.)
- **Write button text that feels natural to say.** Mirror the reader's inner speech ("Start my free trial", first person).
- **Bring interactions to touchable areas.** Bottom, right, foreground — where a hand naturally reaches.
- **Prime the motion action in desired behaviors.** Activate the muscles of the act through the language/imagery.
- **Ease the symbolic motion of progress.** Downward/with-gravity motion feels stronger and easier.
- **Keep positive words near brands and actions.** Valence transfers by proximity — positive words beside the CTA rub off on it.
- **Increase the ratio of positive selections.** Partition the choices to bias allocation toward the option you want.
- **Reduce the fluency of rejection options.** Make declining feel slightly unnatural/awkward ("No thanks, I don't want to save money").
- **Counterbalance a desired action.** Understand the equity/decision scale behind a "yes" and tip it.

## Dates & Numbers (4) — the same quantity can feel large/small, near/far
Numeric and temporal judgments are **relative and reference-dependent.** (Time-perception and numerical-estimation research.)
- **Frame waiting periods in calendar dates.** "By the 14th" feels sooner than "in two weeks."
- **Frame deadlines with remaining time.** Durations motivate more than fixed dates.
- **Keep waiting periods from passing a round number.** 1:58 feels sooner than 2:01 — staying below a round threshold matters.
- **"125% more" feels like "25% more".** The off-by-100% bias: readers drop the leading 100 in relative-change percentages. Phrase increases to exploit or avoid it.

## How these connect to stance (for auditing)
Framing is largely a choice of what to **proclaim**, **entertain**, or scale up/down
(**graduation**); a CTA is a **directive**, often softened by a **commissive** (a
guarantee); a number is a **graduation** device. See `references/stance-vocabulary.md`.
