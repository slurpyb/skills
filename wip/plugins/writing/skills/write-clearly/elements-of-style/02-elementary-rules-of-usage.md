# Rules of usage

Apply the repository's language and formatting conventions first. Use this reference when local guidance does not decide the issue.

## Possessives

Form the possessive singular with apostrophe-s unless a proper name or local style requires another form.

- `the request's headers`
- `the class's constructor`
- `the users' settings`

Possessive pronouns do not use apostrophes: `its`, `theirs`, `yours`.

## Series

Use a serial comma in a list of three or more items unless local style says otherwise.

> The result includes the status, body, and retry delay.

The comma prevents the final two items from appearing to form a pair.

## Parenthetical information

Use paired commas, parentheses, or dashes for nonessential information. Do not insert only one side of a pair.

> The cache, which is rebuilt on startup, contains no user data.

Do not surround information that identifies the subject:

> The cache that contains user data is encrypted.

The first sentence refers to one already identified cache. The second identifies which cache is encrypted.

## Independent clauses

Join independent clauses with one of these forms:

- a period;
- a semicolon;
- a comma followed by a coordinating conjunction.

> The write committed. The projection update failed.

> The write committed; the projection update failed.

> The write committed, but the projection update failed.

Do not join independent clauses with a comma alone.

## Fragments

Use a complete sentence when the text makes a claim. Fragments are acceptable in headings, labels, table cells, parameter descriptions, and intentionally terse interface text when their role is clear.

Do not use fragments to manufacture emphasis in ordinary explanatory prose.

## Introductory modifiers

Attach an introductory phrase to the grammatical subject that performs its action.

Unclear:

> After validating the token, the request continues.

Clear:

> After validating the token, the middleware continues the request.

The request did not validate the token; the middleware did.

## Agreement and pronouns

Match verbs and pronouns to their antecedents. Singular “they” is standard when a person's gender is unknown, irrelevant, or nonbinary.

> Each reviewer records their decision.

Rewrite when a pronoun could refer to more than one noun.

## Commas after introductory material

Use a comma after an introductory clause and after a phrase long enough that the sentence would otherwise be misread.

> When the lease expires, another worker can claim the task.

> After the final retry the task fails.

Short introductory phrases do not always need a comma. Follow local style and add the comma when it clarifies the sentence.

## Colons and semicolons

Use a colon after a complete clause to introduce an explanation, list, or example.

> The parser accepts two forms: a calendar date and an offset timestamp.

Use a semicolon to connect closely related independent clauses or to separate list items that already contain commas.

## Quotation marks and code spans

Use quotation marks for quoted prose and terms discussed as language. Use code formatting for identifiers, literals, commands, paths, and syntax.

> The API returns reason `not_found`, not the phrase “record missing.”

Do not use quotation marks merely to distance the writer from an imprecise term. Replace the term or define it.

## Capitalization

Follow the official capitalization of products, protocols, and identifiers. Use sentence case for headings unless local style specifies another convention.

Do not capitalize a common noun to make it appear important.

## Final check

Punctuation should make sentence structure clear. If a sentence needs several marks to remain understandable, revise the sentence before adjusting its punctuation.
